Estudios de Mercado I · Tema 3 — Triangulation worksheet

What you do: bring together what your netnography, focus groups and in-depth interviews found, and see where they point the same way. The two methods where you asked the questions carry the weight; the netnography — unprompted, but posted by a self-selected, loud minority — is discounted for its bias. And if the online talk tells a very different story, it is reported as a separate lens, not blended into the findings.

The question — the student night out. Client: a new events / nightlife app that wants to win the student segment. Research question: how do students decide where to go out — music, venue, people, budget — and what makes a night feel “worth it”?

1 Bring the three analyses in

Each study arrives as its map plus its evidence. The map is the backbone — it shows each study's themes at a glance, so the three can be laid side by side. Two of the three studies is the minimum; all three is the exercise.

What to paste in each card — in this order:
  1. The map code — the ```mermaid mindmap block from that studio's AI answer. This is the key part: the card shows the themes it finds in it.
  2. The themes with their quotes — the AI's themes, each with its two real quotes.
  3. Your read — which themes held up and which you cut, plus the bias you noted (the thread's, the group's, or the interviewer's).
How: open the studio in the same browser you used (your work is saved there), click the green “AI answer pasted” line to open the box, select all (Ctrl/Cmd+A), copy and paste here — that brings the map and the themes together. Then copy your read and bias boxes below it.

3 Triangulate — the AI builds the convergence grid

  
The prompt carries the three analyses, the biases you ticked and the weights. It asks for one shared list of themes and a grid: for each theme, does each method support it, hint at it, stay silent or contradict it?

  

    The method — triangulation (3.7, credibility):
    
    
      
  1. Harmonise — the same idea often has a different name in each study; merge those into one theme.
    2. 
      
  2. Grid — mark each theme against each method: + supports · ~ hints · 0 silent · x contradicts.
    3. 
      
  3. Weigh — two primary methods agreeing is strong; the netnography adds discounted corroboration.
    4. 
      
  4. Test the netnography — if most of what it says is not confirmed by what you heard in person, it diverges: report it separately as an online lens, and explain why.
    5. 
      
  5. Decide — and name what a survey must now measure.
